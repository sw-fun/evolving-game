Game concepts inspired by your castle siege image
The image is particularly well suited to a family of related strategy, puzzle, and exploration games sharing the same world, map, assets, and simulation engine.

Rather than building one enormous game, I'd consider creating several smaller, independently playable games that progressively introduce mechanics. Some could eventually be combined into a larger RTS.

The image already contains most of the elements needed: castle walls, defensive towers, siege engines, dragons, magic portals, soldiers, bridges, cliffs, projectiles, and multiple elevations.

Tower Defense + resource management

Defend the castle against waves of infantry, siege engines, and dragons. Place archers, ballistae, magical towers, and barricades. Upgrade towers and repair walls between attacks.

2. Siege Commander

RTS + tactical combat

Command either attackers or defenders. Position infantry, archers, cavalry, and siege engines. Capture bridges, breach walls, and control magical towers.

Distinctive mechanic: The battlefield has multiple elevations, with terrain affecting visibility, range, and movement.

3. Rune Keep

Logic puzzle + programmable mechanisms

Activate colored runes in the correct sequence to power castle defenses. Route magical energy between towers, gates, and portals while avoiding overloaded circuits.

Distinctive mechanic: Rune networks function like programmable logic circuits, allowing puzzles involving gates, state machines, and timing.

4. The Shattered Kingdom

World exploration + adventure

Explore the castle, cliffs, underground tunnels, ruined watchtowers, and floating islands. Discover secret passages, collect artifacts, and unlock portals.

Distinctive mechanic: The same world can be explored before, during, and after the siege, with the environment changing between periods.

5. Siege Engineer

Physics + engineering puzzle

Build and configure catapults, trebuchets, and ballistae. Adjust launch angles, counterweights, and projectile types to destroy specific targets with limited ammunition.

Distinctive mechanic: Structural damage matters: collapse a bridge, topple a tower, or break a wall's supporting foundation.

6. Dragonfire Tactics

Turn-based tactics + elemental magic

Control small squads of heroes, mages, archers, and dragons on a grid. Combine spells with terrain and environmental hazards.

Distinctive mechanic: Fire spreads, ice blocks movement, lightning chains through units, and wind alters projectile trajectories.

4. The more interesting possibility: a programmable siege simulator
Given your work with Rust, X_eTaL, COR24, FORTH, and FPGA simulations, there's another direction that could distinguish these games from conventional fantasy titles.

What if players could program the castle's defenses?

Instead of simply placing a tower and watching it fire, a player could define its behavior using a small scripting language or visual logic editor.

For example:

IF enemy.distance < 8
   AND enemy.type == DRAGON
THEN
   tower.weapon = ICE
   tower.target = enemy
   tower.fire()
You could offer three progressively more sophisticated control modes:

Manual: Players select towers and targets directly.

Rule-based: Players define simple conditions and actions.

Programmable: Players write scripts that control towers, units, gates, and siege engines.

That opens the door to a seventh game, Siege Automata, combining tower defense, programming puzzles, and autonomous agents.

It could even support competitive programming challenges: two players design defense algorithms, then compare how their castles perform against identical attack waves.

5. Suggested Rust-first architecture
I would separate the deterministic game simulation from the renderer and user interface.

Game frontends

Tower Defense · RTS · Puzzle · Explorer · Tactics

Shared Rust simulation engine

ECS · pathfinding · combat · AI · physics · scripting · event log

Rendering adapters

Bevy / wgpu / WebGPU

Data and rules

RON / TOML / X_eTaL

Some important architectural decisions:

Deterministic simulation: Fixed timestep, seeded random numbers, replayable events. Useful for testing AI, recording battles, and reproducing bugs.

Isometric rendering: A 2D tile-based world is sufficient for the first few games; full 3D can come later.

Data-driven mechanics: Define towers, units, projectiles, spells, and maps as data rather than embedding their properties in Rust code.

Headless execution: Run thousands of battles without graphics for balancing, AI experiments, and performance testing.

WASM support: Publish small browser-playable demos while retaining native builds.

Bevy is a reasonable choice for ECS and rendering, although a smaller custom simulation core with a separate renderer could offer better portability and isolation.

7. Three directions I'd especially consider
Best conventional game: Castle Defense

The clearest interpretation of the artwork, easiest to explain to players, and a good foundation for subsequent games.

Most distinctive: Siege Automata

Combines strategy, programming, logic puzzles, and autonomous defense systems. Especially suitable for experimentation with X_eTaL or FORTH.

Most ambitious: Siege Commander

A full RTS featuring asymmetric attackers and defenders, destructible terrain, magic, dragons, and sophisticated unit AI.

One additional idea worth considering is making the castle itself a persistent simulation. The player could build and program its defenses in one game, test them in a tower defense game, attack them in an RTS, and explore the resulting ruins in an adventure game. All four experiences would use the same world state.

That would turn the image into more than a setting for a single game: it would become a small, extensible game platform.

One design decision would significantly affect the architecture: Do you envision the final game looking essentially like this image—richly illustrated, fixed-camera isometric 2D—or do you want a true 3D world that players can rotate, zoom into, and explore?
